Dememoria

A neuropsychology and cognitive health project focused on cognitive assessment, dementia, emotional support and clinically meaningful care.

Dememoria brings together clinical practice, cognitive intervention, professional education and public communication around brain health.

01

Overview

Dememoria is a professional project centred on neuropsychology, cognitive health and the clinical realities of cognitive decline.

It was developed from direct experience with older adults, people living with cognitive impairment and dementia, and their families.

The project combines assessment, intervention, emotional care and public communication with a strong emphasis on clinical usefulness and accessibility.

02

Origin

Dememoria emerged from years of professional work in neuropsychology and dementia care.

Clinical practice repeatedly highlighted the same difficulty: cognitive problems are rarely experienced as isolated test scores.

Memory changes, loss of autonomy, family stress, uncertainty and emotional adjustment are usually intertwined.

Dememoria was created to approach cognitive health from this broader perspective.

03

Clinical focus

The clinical model behind Dememoria is based on integrating cognitive, emotional, functional and contextual information.

Cognition

Memory, attention, executive functions, language, visuospatial abilities and other cognitive domains.

Functioning

The impact of cognitive change on autonomy, routines, decision-making and everyday activities.

Emotional health

Anxiety, grief, fear, adjustment and the emotional meaning of cognitive change.

Context

Family dynamics, support networks, living environment and care needs.

04

Neuropsychological assessment

Assessment is one of the central components of Dememoria.

The aim is not simply to obtain scores, but to understand cognitive functioning within the person's broader clinical and functional context.

Clinical interview

Exploration of history, symptoms, daily functioning, emotional state and relevant contextual factors.

Cognitive testing

Selection of neuropsychological tools according to the referral question and individual characteristics.

Functional interpretation

Integration of test performance with autonomy and everyday behaviour.

Clinical formulation

Development of working hypotheses that integrate cognitive, emotional and contextual information.

05

Cognitive and psychological intervention

Dememoria understands cognitive intervention as more than completing exercises.

The therapeutic process may include cognitive stimulation, compensatory strategies, psychoeducation, emotional support and adaptation to changes in everyday functioning.

Cognitive stimulation

Structured activities designed around relevant cognitive processes and individual abilities.

Compensatory strategies

Use of routines, environmental supports and practical tools to facilitate everyday functioning.

Psychoeducation

Clear information about cognitive change, dementia and realistic expectations.

Emotional support

Psychological intervention when cognitive difficulties are accompanied by anxiety, grief, uncertainty or loss of confidence.

06

Families and care

Cognitive decline affects more than one person.

Families often need information, support and practical tools for understanding behavioural changes, communicating more effectively and adapting care.

Dememoria therefore treats family support as part of the wider clinical context rather than as a secondary issue.

07

Education and communication

Another important dimension of Dememoria is translating neuropsychological knowledge into clear and useful information.

Cognitive health is often surrounded by fear, misinformation and overly simplistic messages.

Professional communication can help people understand symptoms, assessment, dementia and prevention without creating false certainty or unnecessary alarm.

08

Digital development

Dememoria has progressively expanded beyond direct clinical care into digital content, professional resources and educational materials.

This digital layer creates a bridge between clinical experience and scalable knowledge.

It also provided part of the conceptual foundation for later projects focused more directly on digital cognitive technology.

09

Principles

Person-centred

Cognitive difficulties should be understood in relation to the person, not only the diagnosis.

Clinically grounded

Recommendations should remain connected to assessment, observed functioning and realistic goals.

Clear

Complex neuropsychological information should be communicated without unnecessary jargon or false precision.

Ethical

Privacy, dignity, informed decision-making and professional responsibility remain central.

10

Future direction

Dememoria continues to function as the clinical foundation of my work in cognitive health.

Its future development is increasingly connected with digital education, structured cognitive resources and interdisciplinary work across neuropsychology, neuroscience and technology.

The project also provides a clinical reference point for evaluating whether new technologies remain meaningful for real people and real cognitive needs.

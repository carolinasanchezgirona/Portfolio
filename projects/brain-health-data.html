Overview

This project documents my first computational explorations at the intersection of biomedical engineering, neuroscience and data science.

The aim is not simply to produce visualisations or code, but to understand how biological and clinical information can be represented, analysed and interpreted responsibly.

Each experiment will include the original question, the dataset or signal used, the analytical process, the limitations of the approach and a reflection on its possible relevance to brain health.

Motivation

Clinical neuropsychology produces complex information about cognition, behaviour and everyday functioning. Biomedical engineering and data science offer new ways to organise, process and visualise that information.

However, technical sophistication does not automatically create clinical meaning. Data may appear precise while still being incomplete, biased or disconnected from the person behind the measurements.

This project allows me to develop computational skills while maintaining a critical perspective on interpretation, transparency and responsible innovation.

Objectives

  • Develop practical skills in Python and scientific computing.
  • Explore the visualisation of biomedical and neurological signals.
  • Learn the foundations of signal processing and data analysis.
  • Create clear and reproducible computational experiments.
  • Examine the limitations and possible biases of analytical methods.
  • Connect technical results with meaningful questions about brain health.
  • Explore responsible and explainable artificial intelligence.

Future plans

As my technical knowledge develops, this project may include:

  • Python notebooks using public or synthetic biomedical datasets
  • Visualisation of heart rate and pulse signals
  • Introductory EEG signal analysis
  • Frequency-domain and time-domain visualisations
  • Noise filtering and signal preprocessing
  • Exploratory analysis of cognitive and neurological data
  • Simple machine-learning experiments
  • Explainable artificial intelligence examples
  • Reflections on bias, validity and clinical interpretation

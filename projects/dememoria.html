Dememoria

A neuropsychology and cognitive health project focused on cognitive assessment, dementia, emotional support and clinically meaningful care.

Dememoria brings together clinical practice, cognitive intervention, professional education and communication around cognition and brain health.

01

Overview

Dememoria is a professional project centred on neuropsychology, cognitive health and the clinical realities of cognitive decline.

It was developed from direct clinical experience with older adults, people living with cognitive impairment and dementia, and their families.

The project combines assessment, intervention, emotional care and communication with a strong emphasis on clinical usefulness, individual functioning and person-centred care.

02

Origin

Dememoria emerged from professional work in neuropsychology, cognitive assessment and dementia care.

Clinical practice repeatedly highlighted the same issue: cognitive difficulties are rarely experienced as isolated test scores.

Memory changes, loss of autonomy, emotional adjustment, uncertainty and family stress are often deeply interconnected.

Dememoria was developed to approach cognitive health from this broader clinical perspective.

03

Clinical focus

The clinical perspective behind Dememoria integrates cognitive, emotional, functional and contextual information.

Cognition

Memory, attention, executive functions, language, visuospatial abilities and other cognitive domains.

Functioning

The impact of cognitive change on autonomy, routines, decision-making and everyday activities.

Emotional health

Anxiety, grief, fear, adjustment and the emotional meaning associated with cognitive change.

Context

Family dynamics, support networks, living environment and individual care needs.

04

Neuropsychological assessment

Assessment is one of the central components of Dememoria.

The objective is not simply to obtain cognitive scores, but to understand performance within the person's broader clinical, emotional and functional context.

Clinical interview

Exploration of clinical history, symptoms, daily functioning, emotional state and relevant contextual factors.

Cognitive testing

Selection and interpretation of neuropsychological tools according to the referral question and individual characteristics.

Functional interpretation

Integration of cognitive performance with autonomy, behaviour and everyday functioning.

Clinical formulation

Development of working hypotheses integrating cognitive, emotional, functional and contextual information.

05

Cognitive and psychological intervention

Dememoria approaches cognitive intervention as more than the completion of cognitive exercises.

Depending on individual needs, intervention may include cognitive stimulation, compensatory strategies, psychoeducation, emotional support and adaptation to changes in everyday functioning.

Cognitive stimulation

Structured activities designed around relevant cognitive processes, individual abilities and therapeutic objectives.

Compensatory strategies

Routines, environmental supports and practical tools intended to facilitate everyday functioning and autonomy.

Psychoeducation

Clear information about cognitive change, dementia, assessment and realistic expectations.

Emotional support

Psychological intervention when cognitive difficulties are accompanied by anxiety, grief, uncertainty or loss of confidence.

06

Families and care

Cognitive decline affects more than one person.

Families frequently need information, emotional support and practical strategies for understanding behavioural changes, communicating effectively and adapting care.

Dememoria therefore considers family support part of the wider clinical context rather than a secondary component of care.

07

Education and communication

Another dimension of Dememoria involves translating neuropsychological knowledge into clear, rigorous and useful information.

Cognitive health and dementia are frequently surrounded by fear, misinformation and overly simplistic messages.

Professional communication can help people understand symptoms, assessment, cognitive change and dementia without creating false certainty or unnecessary alarm.

08

Digital development

Dememoria has progressively expanded beyond direct clinical care into digital content, educational materials and professional resources.

This digital dimension creates a bridge between clinical experience and more scalable forms of knowledge dissemination.

It has also contributed to the conceptual foundation of later projects exploring the intersection between neuropsychology, cognitive health and technology.

09

Principles

Person-centred

Cognitive difficulties should be understood in relation to the person, their history and their everyday functioning, rather than through diagnosis alone.

Clinically grounded

Assessment and intervention should remain connected to observed functioning, clinical reasoning and realistic goals.

Clear

Complex neuropsychological information should be communicated clearly without sacrificing scientific rigour.

Ethical

Privacy, dignity, informed decision-making and professional responsibility remain central throughout the project.

10

Future direction

Dememoria continues to represent the clinical foundation of my work in cognitive health.

Its future development is increasingly connected with digital education, structured cognitive resources and interdisciplinary work across neuropsychology, neuroscience and technology.

The project also provides a clinical reference point for evaluating whether emerging technologies remain meaningful for real people, real environments and real cognitive needs.
